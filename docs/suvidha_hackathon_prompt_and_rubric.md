# Suvidha AI Virtual Hackathon 2026  
**Prompt + Full Rubric**

---

## The Challenge

**Build something that makes learning accessible to someone who is currently locked out of it.**

Not education in general, and not accessibility in general.  
We mean the **intersection**: someone who wants to learn but can’t, because of:

- how the material is delivered  
- what language it’s in  
- what it assumes about their body or brain  
- or what resources they don’t have  

The best projects won’t be the most technically complex ones.  
They’ll be the ones where you can **name the person you built for** and **show exactly what changed for them**.

A working prototype that removes **one narrow barrier** beats an ambitious demo that removes nothing.

---

## What You Submit

Three items, submitted through Devpost before the deadline. All three are read by the panel.

### Item 01 — Live Demo Link
A working, publicly accessible version.  
Judges need to open it and use it without setup, installs, or credentials.  
If your project truly cannot be hosted, submit a video demo and tell us why.

### Item 02 — GitHub Repository
Public, with a README covering:
- Setup
- Your stack
- What is fully built versus mocked

Being honest about scope helps you here. Judges reward clarity over overclaiming.

### Item 03 — One Pager
A single page describing your project. Format is up to you, so this is where you can get creative.  

At minimum it must cover:
- Who you built for
- What barrier you are removing
- How it works

## Build Rules

- **Nothing prebuilt**  
  Everything you submit has to be written during the build window, after the prompt is released on August 15. Bringing in work you started earlier is not allowed.

- **Open source is fine**  
  Libraries, frameworks, APIs and public models are all fair game. Use whatever helps you build, including but not limited to Featherless AI. What matters is that the project itself is yours.

- **Commit history reviewed**  
  Judges check your repository’s commit timeline to confirm the work happened inside the window. Commit regularly as you go rather than pushing everything at the end. A single commit before the deadline is hard to verify and looks worse than messy incremental history.

- **One team per person**  
  Joining more than one team is an automatic disqualification. Every team the person appears on is removed from judging, including teams that were unaware of the overlap.

---

## Required Disclosures

- **AI tools**  
  Every model, API, and coding assistant used to build or run your project. Name the tool and say what you used it for. This includes models called through Featherless AI. Name the specific models.

- **Datasets**  
  Every dataset, with its source and licence. Public, scraped, synthetic, and self collected all need to be named.

Using AI tools and public data is expected and encouraged. Judges are assessing what you built on top of them, not whether you used them. Disclosures are assessed pass or fail rather than scored. Undisclosed use is grounds for disqualification.

## Scoring

Scored out of **50 points** across four categories.  
Judges select exactly one band per category. There are no intermediate scores.

| Category              | What it answers                                      | Weight | Points |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------------------------|--------|--------|
| Problem Relevance     | Is there a real person locked out of learning, and does this reach them? | 30%    | 15     |
| Originality           | Has anyone approached it this way before?            | 30%    | 15     |
| Execution             | Does it actually work?                               | 20%    | 10     |
| Presentation          | Could a judge understand it in two minutes?          | 20%    | 10     |
| **Total**             |                                                      | 100%   | **50** |

**Read order**  
Judges read your one pager first, then open your demo, then check the repository. Put your best explanation in the one pager.

**Broken links**  
A demo that does not load costs you under Execution. Test your link in a fresh browser before you submit.

**Ties**  
Broken by Problem Relevance first, then Originality, then panel discussion.

**Finality**  
The decision of the judges and of Suvidha International is final. Appeals and score reviews are not accepted.

## The Rubric in Full

### Problem Relevance — 30% / 15 pts  
**Who you built for, and how well you understand their barrier**

| Score | Band       | Description |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| 15    | Exemplary  | The barrier is specific and well researched, ideally informed by talking to or being someone affected by it. Every design decision traces back to it. |
| 12    | Strong     | Names a specific user and describes the barrier concretely. The design choices clearly follow from that understanding. |
| 9     | Developing | Sits in the intersection. A user group is named and the barrier is plausible, but the understanding stays surface level. |
| 6     | Emerging   | Touches education or accessibility but not both. The user is a broad category. The barrier is asserted rather than examined. |
| 3     | Beginning  | No meaningful link to the intersection. No identifiable user, or the barrier described does not actually block learning. |

### Originality — 30% / 15 pts  
**Novelty of the problem, or of your approach to it**

| Score | Band       | Description |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| 15    | Exemplary  | Surprising and well argued. Reframes the problem in a way the judges had not considered, and the reframing holds up. |
| 12    | Strong     | Genuinely fresh. Either an underserved problem or a much better approach to a known one, with reasoning behind the choice. |
| 9     | Developing | Known problem space, but your framing, user choice, or approach contributes something of your own. |
| 6     | Emerging   | Familiar idea executed in a familiar way. Little separates it from tools the judges could already name. |
| 3     | Beginning  | A direct clone of an existing tool, or a generic build with the prompt attached afterward. |

### Execution — 20% / 10 pts  
**Whether it actually works, end to end**

| Score | Band       | Description |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| 10    | Exemplary  | Polished and dependable. You could hand it to your target user today. The README makes the built versus mocked line unmistakable. |
| 8     | Strong     | Works reliably end to end. Edge cases handled thoughtfully. Repository is clean and honest about scope. |
| 6     | Developing | The core feature works. Rough edges or some friction getting started, but the central idea is demonstrably real. |
| 4     | Emerging   | Partially working. The core feature is mocked or breaks on ordinary input. Repository shows minimal implementation. |
| 2     | Beginning  | Demo does not load or is not accessible. Repository is empty, unrelated, or has no work from the event window. |

### Presentation — 20% / 10 pts  
**How fast a judge can understand what you made**

| Score | Band       | Description |
|-------|------------|-------------|
| 10    | Exemplary  | Lands the problem and the solution immediately, and sticks. Creative use of the open format that serves the message instead of decorating it. |
| 8     | Strong     | Communicates quickly and cleanly. Easy to evaluate at speed. The one pager format is used deliberately. |
| 6     | Developing | Clear enough. Covers the user, the barrier, and how it works. Disclosures present and complete. |
| 4     | Emerging   | Hard to follow. Judges have to dig through the repository or demo to work out the basic idea. |
| 2     | Beginning  | One pager missing, unreadable, or does not convey what the project does. Disclosures absent. |
